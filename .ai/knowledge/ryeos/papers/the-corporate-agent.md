# Paper 3 — The Corporate Agent

Working notes, not a draft. Downstream of execution identity and attributable
testimony. Revised September 2026: the corporate analogy motivates governance
contracts; it does not confer personhood or replace process enforcement.

## Thesis

> A durable mandate and attributable body of work can survive replacement of
> the models, processes and authorised custodians that perform it.

An organisation survives changes of personnel because its commitments and
authority are not identical to those personnel. That is a useful design model
for long-running agent work. It is not a claim about consciousness, rights,
legal personhood or an already implemented universal agent-key hierarchy.

## The one claim

Separating an enduring mandate from its executor makes delegation, succession
and responsibility explicit design questions. RyeOS's execution records and
authority contracts provide a foundation; they do not automatically settle
those questions.

## Custodial identity

A model need not possess a private signing key to act through a governed
execution. A custodian can exercise authority on its behalf under an admitted
contract. The record must distinguish requesting principal, executing node,
publisher, workload authority and relevant runtime identity.

"The model is an officer" is an analogy for bounded participation, not an
assertion that the model itself signed every act. A key does not establish
exclusive custody, personal identity or an unbroken unique autobiography.

## Succession

Retained commitments and work can remain meaningful after an executor changes.
That does not make all replacements equivalent or automatically authorised.
A model/runtime change may require a new admission or linked work rather than
editing a frozen execution.

Key loss, compromise, rotation and custodial transfer require separate
contracts. Historical signature validity, current trust and present permission
to continue are distinct questions. The future key-lifecycle design owns those
decisions; this paper does not claim they are already solved.

## Standing and delegation

Here, **standing** means the design possibility of being bound to a mandate
and attributable acts under an explicit policy. Trust and authority are granted
decisions, not inevitable consequences of possessing a signed history.

An organisational reading of the substrate is useful:

- Delegation records name scope and its authorising principal.
- Child execution must respect inherited authority ceilings.
- Execution lineage records how work relates.
- Required checks and disposition distinguish completion from acceptance.

The grant graph is not the whole organisation. It does not automatically
encode responsibility, employment, consent, incentives or legal liability.

## Responsibility and the warrant chain

A **warrant chain** connects an act to its invocation authority and the
principals granting it. It supports questions such as who requested an action,
which grant admitted it, and which executor reported its outcome.

A complete walk requires the relevant evidence to remain available. It is not
proof of external causation, a full account of unrecorded activity, or a legal
judgment. Other systems can retain equivalent evidence; this is not impossible
on session-oriented infrastructure.

## Governance and control

Long-running autonomy needs both:

- Enforcement: process confinement, credential boundaries, action gates,
  cancellation and resource limits.
- Governance: explicit mandates, review, renewal, revocation and separate
  result acceptance.

A record of harm does not prevent harm. More capable executors do not remove
the need for isolation. Conversely, a sandbox does not define what counts as
successful work or who may publish a candidate.

Self-hosted development makes the distinction concrete: a candidate may propose
a host change without acquiring authority to install itself or redefine its
own acceptance procedure.

## The price of continuity

Attributable records can expose errors and make contradictory claims easier
to inspect. They can also expose sensitive information, preserve poisoning,
or support unfair judgments based on obsolete context.

The person must be able to revise current beliefs and permissions without
falsifying retained history. Retention and forgetting have explicit limits.
Signed records do not prevent omission, alternative signed branches or erasure
of every available copy; stronger completeness claims need additional evidence.

## Reputation and exchange

Portable work evidence can support evaluation across sites and organisations.
A recipient still needs a named evaluation procedure, coverage of relevant
attempts, trust in the evidence and awareness of correlated reviewers.

A signed portfolio is not a universal reputation score. Selective disclosure,
cheap new identities, collusion and key compromise remain problems.
This is a potential application of the substrate, not a qualified agent market.

## Demonstrations to qualify

- Trace one delegated act through exact authority and durable result.
- Replace an executor at an authorised boundary without losing the mandate.
- Refuse stale or revoked authority while preserving historical evidence.
- Distinguish completed work, independently checked work and accepted work.
- Exercise correction and retention without implying permanent surveillance.

## Guardrails

Keep the organisational analogy separate from legal and moral claims.
Preserve distinct identity roles; introduce no new hierarchy by analogy.
Governance complements enforcement. Succession, availability and accountability
remain conditional on their actual custody, retention and qualification rules.
